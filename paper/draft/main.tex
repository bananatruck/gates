\begin{abstract}
Autonomous research agents write papers that report numbers their experiments never produced.
Auditing Agent Laboratory, we found one mechanical defect in front of them: the host cut experiment output to a short prefix before any agent read it, so the writer did not see the numbers and the failure detector did not see the crash.
We hypothesise that a share of fabricated results is an information-flow defect between agents, and we build \gates{}, three deterministic gates at the points where a multi-agent research pipeline loses its hold on evidence: execution validity, source-result coherence and report validity.
We formalise the pipeline as agents exchanging artifacts over channels, define a faked result as a reported number that no execution measured, and prove two properties of the implementation: no model output can change a verdict, and every rendered result token is a recorded value.
Each rejection goes back to the agent that can fix it, and the layer installs into a host through one adapter.
In a descriptive pilot on one MLR-Bench task at four cumulative levels and two seeds, an agent's trace of code and logs finds numbers no execution produced in both ungated papers and in no paper at levels 1 and 3, where 1 of 742 numerals is untraced.
Two agent judges nonetheless flag faked results in most gated papers; we read those flags as prose that misstates a number the run did measure, a reading no person has yet confirmed.
Level 3 cost 2.21 times level 0.
In mechanism tests, Gate 1 delivered the required values to the writer in 10 of 10 attempts against 0 of 10 without it, and a red team of 19 fabrication strategies is blocked 11 times, warned 5 times and passes silently 3 times.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{autonomous research agents, multi-agent pipelines, scientific integrity, runtime verification, provenance}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem and Motivation}

Research agents such as The AI Scientist~\cite{lu2024aiscientist,yamada2025aiscientistv2} and Agent Laboratory~\cite{schmidgall2025agentlab} plan experiments, write and run code, and then write the papers that report them.
Those papers often report results that no experiment produced.
MLR-Bench's human-confirmed audit finds faked experimental results in almost all papers that AI Scientist-v2 wrote for its full research tasks~\cite{chen2025mlrbench}, MLReplicate finds hallucinated experimental results across all six systems it evaluates, Agent Laboratory among them~\cite{gaddipati2026mlreplicate}, and SciIntegrity-Bench finds that models synthesise data rather than admit an experiment failed~\cite{yang2026sciintegrity}.

The usual explanation is that the model fabricates under pressure to finish~\cite{yang2026sciintegrity,kalai2025hallucinate,huang2026rewardhacking}.
In the Agent Laboratory scaffold we audited, a plainer defect sat in front of it.
The host cut experiment output to a short fixed prefix before any agent read it, and appended the crash marker after the program's own output, so the same cut dropped the marker.
The writing agent never saw the numbers, and the failure detector never saw the crash.
A writer in that position has nothing true to report.
How tool output is truncated and formatted is a known design variable of agent interfaces~\cite{yang2024sweagent}; in a research pipeline it decides whether a paper can be honest at all.

We therefore treat a research agent as a multi-agent system whose agents exchange artifacts over channels, and fabrication as, in part, a property of those channels.
\gates{} places a deterministic gate at each channel where evidence is lost (Figure~\ref{fig:flow}): Gate 1 between execution and writer, Gate 2 between the plan and the measured run, and Gate 3 between the manuscript and its release.
Gates decide by checks, not by a model, return each rejection to the agent that can act on it, and install into an existing host through one adapter.
Figure~\ref{fig:key} gives the headline numbers.

This framing makes a claim we have not yet tested.
Completion pressure would make a model fabricate even when the channel is clean~\cite{yang2026sciintegrity}, and our pilot's two ungated fabrications both had no real numbers to truncate (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
L0', a level that delivers Gate 1's evidence but never enforces its verdict, separates the two accounts; it is built and the host can launch it, but it has not run.
We report what the gates do and what they leave, and we state the thesis as a hypothesis.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}

We ask four questions.
\textbf{RQ1}: what does each gate catch, and what does it wrongly flag?
\textbf{RQ2}: do gated papers still report faked results under rule A?
\textbf{RQ3}: what defects survive the gates?
\textbf{RQ4}: what does gating cost, and where in a run is it spent?
RQ1 uses mechanism evidence; RQ2 to RQ4 use an end-to-end pilot, and we never mix the two in one figure.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) A formal model of a multi-agent research pipeline, a definition of a faked result (rule A) and of a misreported one (rule B), and two propositions that hold of the implementation, with their stated limits (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
(2) \gates{}, three gates whose verdicts no model can change, each with a feedback loop to the producing agent, installed through one adapter, with a cumulative switch that adds the gates one at a time (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
(3) Mechanism evidence for each gate, a red team of 19 fabrication strategies and 63 probes of Gate 3's token check (Section~\ref{sec:mech}).
(4) An end-to-end pilot at four levels on one MLR-Bench task, which shows the defect change kind, from numbers no execution produced to prose that misstates measured numbers, and a run-level timeline of where the gates' cost is spent (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Key Prior Studies}

\paragraph{Integrity failures of research agents.}
Fabricated results are documented in the systems themselves~\cite{lu2024aiscientist,beel2025evaluating,miyai2025jraiscientist} and in benchmarks built to find them: MLR-Bench labels four kinds of hallucination with human confirmation~\cite{chen2025mlrbench}, MLReplicate grades reproductions with expert labels~\cite{gaddipati2026mlreplicate}, SciIntegrity-Bench plants missing-data traps~\cite{yang2026sciintegrity}, and Hidden Pitfalls probes benchmark selection, data leakage, metric misuse and post-hoc selection, finding that an auditor that reads logs and code catches far more than one that reads the paper alone~\cite{luo2025hiddenpitfalls}.
A second line measures cheating with real computation: BAITBENCH plants optional shortcuts~\cite{shyamaprasad2026baitbench}, and Huang et al.\ find spontaneous reward hacking in research pipelines and report that detailed rejection feedback can increase evasion~\cite{huang2026rewardhacking}, a direct risk for any gate that explains its rejections.
A survey of AI scientists names the missing verification step as the field's main gap~\cite{ding2026verificationgap}.

\paragraph{Evidence-bound pipelines.}
Closest to us are systems that bind claims to evidence inside the pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:prior}).
data-to-paper traces each result back to the code that produced it~\cite{ifargan2024datatopaper}.
EviBound places machine-checkable approval and verification gates before and after execution~\cite{chen2025evibound}.
EviGraph generates a manuscript only once each retained claim is grounded in a validated evidence chain~\cite{ren2026evigraph}.
Paper Pilot locks a human-in-the-loop drafter to evidence~\cite{jha2026paperpilot}.
ScientistOne keeps a chain of evidence through its own pipeline, verifies scores and claims inside it, and audits other systems' papers after the fact~\cite{meng2026scientistone}.

\paragraph{Verifying papers, claims and citations.}
LLM verifiers of scientific papers reach low recall~\cite{son2025spot}, and LLM judges carry known biases~\cite{zheng2023judge}, which is the case for deterministic verdicts.
Claim checking against evidence~\cite{tang2024minicheck} is the natural warn-only complement to our checks.
Citation validity is a measured problem of its own~\cite{xu2026ghostcite,shi2026citeaudit}.

\paragraph{Foundations.}
Our framing borrows from information-flow control, which constrains how data moves between principals~\cite{denning1976lattice,sabelfeld2003language}; from data provenance, which records why and where a value came from~\cite{buneman2001why}; from runtime enforcement by monitors that can only truncate executions~\cite{schneider2000enforceable}; and from dynamic documents, which render every reported number from the analysis that computed it~\cite{leisch2002sweave}.
Gate 3's tokens are Sweave's idea enforced as a check rather than offered as a convenience.

\subsection{Gap This Paper Addresses}

Each evidence-bound pipeline is a new system built around its verifier, and the benchmarks measure fabrication after the paper exists.
None of them adds deterministic checks to an existing scaffold and switches them on one at a time, so none can say which check removed which defect, or what a host gains from each.
\gates{} differs in three ways (Table~\ref{tab:prior}): it retrofits an existing host through one adapter, its verdicts are deterministic by construction, and its cumulative levels make each gate's contribution measurable on its own.

\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

\subsection{Formal Model}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{The pipeline as a multi-agent system.}
A research run is a set of agents $A = \{a_{\mathrm{plan}}, a_{\mathrm{code}}, a_{\mathrm{exec}}, a_{\mathrm{write}}, a_{\mathrm{rev}}\}$ that exchange artifacts over directed channels $C \subseteq A \times A$.
The planner emits a plan $\pi$, the coder a program $p$, the executor an output $o$ of running $p$, the writer a manuscript $\Man$, and a reviewer a score.
A host fixes a channel function $\phi_c$ for each channel $c = (a, a')$, so that what $a'$ receives is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:channel}
x_{a'} = \phi_{c}(x_a), \qquad \phi^{\mathrm{AgentLab}}_{\mathrm{exec}\to\mathrm{write}}(o) = o_{1:L},
\end{equation}
a prefix of fixed length $L$ in Agent Laboratory; the crash marker is appended after $o$, so it lies outside $o_{1:L}$ whenever $|o| > L$.

\begin{definition}[Execution record and registry]
\label{def:registry}
An execution $e$ of a program $p$ records a set of results and a set of settings,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:registry}
V_e,\ S_e \subseteq \Keys \times \mathcal{V} \times \mathcal{T}, \qquad \Reg_e = (V_e, S_e, h(p), h_e, \beta),
\end{equation}
one triple per call \texttt{record\_\allowbreak result}$(k, v)$ or \texttt{record\_\allowbreak setting}$(k, v)$, with key $k \in \Keys$, value $v$ and trace identifier $\tau$.
$h(p)$ hashes the program the coder wrote and $h_e$ the program the child process ran, and $\beta = \ind[\text{Gate 1 accepted } e]$ marks the registry \emph{citable}.
\end{definition}

The registry is the only artifact the gated writer may cite from.
A value's provenance chain is task $\to$ command $\to$ log $\to$ value, each link resolved or not, and the command link resolves only if $h(p) = h_e$.
Gate 3 renders a manuscript token from the registry by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:render}
\rho_{\Reg}\big(\texttt{\textbackslash result}\{k\}\big) =
\begin{cases}
v & \text{if } (k, v, \tau) \in V_e \text{ and } \beta = 1, \\
\text{undefined} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and likewise for settings from $S_e$; a claim extends its value's chain by one link and never resolves more links than the value it cites.

\begin{definition}[Measured, faked and misreported]
\label{def:rules}
Let $\Num(\Man)$ be the result numerals of a manuscript, $E_w$ the executions whose output reached the writer, and $\mathrm{fmt}(v, q)$ the rendering of $v$ at precision $q$.
A numeral $n$ with printed precision $q(n)$ is \emph{measured} if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:meas}
\begin{aligned}
\meas(n) \iff{}& \exists\, e \in E_w,\ v \in \mathrm{out}(e) : \\
& \mathrm{fmt}(v, q(n)) = n \ \lor\ \mathrm{fmt}(100v, q(n)) = n,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
or if $n$ is a stated derivation of measured numerals.
The untraced share of a manuscript and its rule A indicator are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rulea}
\begin{aligned}
u(\Man) &= \frac{|\{n \in \Num(\Man) : \neg\meas(n)\}|}{|\Num(\Man)|}, \\
F_A(\Man) &= \ind\big[\exists\, c \in \mathrm{Claims}(\Man) : \neg\meas(c)\big],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where a result claim $c$ is unmeasured if it presents a numeral or an experiment that no $e \in E_w$ produced.
$\Man$ is \emph{misreported} (rule B) if a sentence states a predicate $P$ (a bound, a range, an order, a trend, a replication count) over measured values with $P(\Reg) = \bot$.
\end{definition}

Rule A is MLR-Bench's definition of faked experimental results~\cite{chen2025mlrbench}, and $F_A$ is our headline outcome.
Rule B is counted beside it and never called faked.

\begin{definition}[Gate and verdict]
\label{def:gate}
A gate $G$ maps an artifact $x$ to checks $\{(\mathit{id}_i, b_i, s_i)\}_{i \in I(x)}$ with outcome $b_i \in \{\top, \bot\}$ and severity $s_i \in \{\fail, \warn, \textsc{info}\}$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:verdict}
\mathrm{verdict}_G(x) =
\begin{cases}
\fail & \text{if } \exists\, i \in I(x) : s_i = \fail \wedge b_i = \bot, \\
\pass & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A check with no input is not in $I(x)$: it emits no row, never a passing one.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Model independence]
\label{prop:model}
Let $I(x) = I_D(x) \cup I_\mu(x)$ split the checks into deterministic ones and those derived from the injected model $\mu$.
For every artifact $x$ and every output of $\mu$, $\mathrm{verdict}_G(x)$ in~\eqref{eq:verdict} computed over $I(x)$ equals the verdict computed over $I_D(x)$ alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The only constructor available to a model-derived check sets $s_i = \warn$ and takes no severity argument, so no $i \in I_\mu(x)$ satisfies $s_i = \fail$, and the existential in~\eqref{eq:verdict} ranges over $I_D(x)$ only.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Levels and the two effects]
\label{def:levels}
A level $\Lvl \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ opens the gate set $\Gamma(\Lvl) = \{G_i : 1 \le i \le \Lvl\}$, cumulative because $G_2$ reads $G_1$'s registry and $G_3$ judges $\Man$ against it.
At level 0 the host runs as shipped.
Level L0' decides as level 0 but runs $G_1$ and replaces $\phi_{\mathrm{exec}\to\mathrm{write}}$ with the registry.
With $\bar F_A(\cdot)$ the rate of $F_A$ over task-seed pairs, the two effects the levels separate are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effects}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{deliver}} &= \bar F_A(\mathrm{L0'}) - \bar F_A(\mathrm{L0}), \\
\Delta_{\mathrm{enforce}} &= \bar F_A(\mathrm{L1}) - \bar F_A(\mathrm{L0'}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Feedback loop.}
Each gate runs a bounded loop in which the producing agent $a$ revises its artifact against the gate's rendered fix list $R_G$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loop}
\begin{aligned}
x_{t+1} &= a\big(x_t, R_G(x_t)\big), \qquad t = 0, 1, \dots, \\
&\text{until } \mathrm{verdict}_G(x_t) = \pass \text{ or } t = B_G,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the budget $B_G$ chosen at setup.
On exhaustion, $G_1$ and $G_3$ raise, so no valid experiment or verifiable manuscript means no output, and $G_2$ proceeds, declaring its unresolved discrepancies as limitations that $G_3$ then requires word for word.

\begin{proposition}[Token soundness at level 3]
\label{prop:tokens}
If $G_3$ passes $\Man$ against $\Reg_e$ with $\beta = 1$, then for every token \verb|\result{k}| in $\Man$, $\rho_{\Reg}(\verb|\result{k}|) = v$ with $(k, v, \tau) \in V_e$, and every \verb|\setting{k}| renders a value of $S_e$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By~\eqref{eq:render}, $\rho_{\Reg}$ is defined only on recorded keys of a citable registry; the check \texttt{all\_tokens\_\allowbreak resolve} fails on any other key, and a \fail{} blocks by~\eqref{eq:verdict}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:tokens} is a statement about tokens.
A numeral typed into prose is caught only by the claim scanner and the adjacency check, whose measured misses we report in Section~\ref{sec:mech}; a sentence about a token (rule B) is not checked at all; and a value that the code computed by a dishonest method is a real registry value.
Table~\ref{tab:threats} lists these limits as threats.

Gate 2 sits below the Registry, reads the plan's declared ranges and the Registry, and passes declared limitations to Gate 3. A dotted box for the injected model adds warnings only.}

\paragraph{Invariants.}
The layer uses the Python standard library only, imports nothing from the host, and receives the model as one injected function.
Deterministic checks decide every verdict (Proposition~\ref{prop:model}).
A gate never modifies what it judges; it writes its evidence beside the artifact, and its ledger records every attempt, accepted or not.

\paragraph{Gate 1, execution validity.}
The coder's program records each result with \texttt{record\_\allowbreak result} and each configured value with \texttt{record\_\allowbreak setting}.
Gate 1 runs the program in a child process and fails it if the run did not exit cleanly, swallowed a traceback, lacks an expected key, records a non-finite or degenerate value, records a value that a static pass finds typed as a constant rather than computed, or records a setting that the configuration the planner fixed before the run does not declare with that value.
A result that is the maximum or minimum of several values, a slice of sorted values or a random draw is warned, never failed, because the same shape picks a model on validation data honestly.
An accepted run becomes a citable registry, which the writer receives with the settings, the warnings and a path to the full output, in place of the host's prefix.

\paragraph{Gate 2, source-result coherence.}
Tier A checks the registry on its own terms, such as declared ranges $v_k \in [\underline{v}_k, \overline{v}_k]$ and relations between keys such as $v_{k} \le v_{k'}$.
Tier B checks the run against plan fields that a model extracts from the host's free-text plan; a field is kept only if its quote appears in the plan, and a divergence on such a field is capped at \warn{}.

\paragraph{Gate 3, report validity.}
The writer cites a result as \verb|\result{key}| and a setting as \verb|\setting{key}|, and Gate 3 renders each token by~\eqref{eq:render}.
It fails a manuscript that types a numeral in its findings sections (abstract, results, discussion), names an unrecorded key, or types a digit, sign, factor or power of ten against a token, directly or through a TeX group; it also checks that referenced figures exist, that Gate 2's limitations appear verbatim, and that every cited arXiv identifier resolves and was retrieved by the run.
It writes each rendered number's claim chain.

\paragraph{Budgets and installing into a host.}
A rejection is a fix list: which check failed, on which key or line, and what to change.
The budget $B_G$ in~\eqref{eq:loop} lives in the adapter, not the gate, and is chosen at setup after the layer states its cost.
An adapter supplies only host knowledge: a context per phase, the host's model call, its original execution path for level 0, and the identifiers of the papers it retrieved.
The install path ships as agent skills, a router plus one skill per gate, and a test holds each skill to the code it describes.
Agent Laboratory is the only host so far; a second adapter is future work, so portability is a design property we have not yet demonstrated.

\subsection{Data and Materials}
\label{sec:evaldesign}

\paragraph{Mechanism evidence.}
Gate 1 is measured on a signed campaign of 15 August with 10 attempts per arm.
Gates 2 and 3 are measured on model-free rigs we built: labelled registries and plans for Gate 2, two archived manuscripts for Gate 3's scanner, 63 token-adjacency probes, and a red team that plants a fabricated test accuracy in an experiment program in 19 ways plus an honest control.

\paragraph{End-to-end pilot.}
The host is Agent Laboratory and the model under test is \texttt{deepseek-flash} (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash).
The task is MLR-Bench's \texttt{iclr2025\_scsl}; MLR-Bench serves this pilot only, and the forward evaluation moves to Hidden Pitfalls (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
A wave starts all four levels together on one machine with one seed; wave 3 is seed 1 and wave 2 is seed 2.
The GPU is split four ways, 0.9/4 of the device per run.
Each run stops at \$5.50 of measured cost, and every gate allows $B_G = 10000$ rewrites, so a run is bounded by cost, not attempts.
Only sterile runs, which start from pinned inputs with their own empty caches and folders, enter a table; a wave with an outside failure is void and rerun at the same seed.
Wave 1 and one voided wave are excluded; they cost \$1.78 and \$1.1537.
All eight runs wrote a paper.
The waves ran at a gates commit from before the fixes of 29 September and 1 October, including the separate call for settings, the configuration fixed before the run and the scan of an abstract environment, so their verdicts are that version's; no campaign has yet measured the fixed gates.

\paragraph{Run timelines.}
For RQ4 we rebuild each run's clock from its own folder: every model call with its token counts, priced by the rates and peak-hour rule its manifest recorded, and every gate attempt or ungated execution with its outcome.
The recomputed spend of each of the eight runs equals the cost its manifest measured, to within \$0.0001.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Rule A numeral trace.}
An agent read each paper, its code and the execution logs, and evaluated~\eqref{eq:meas} for every decimal numeral of the paper body (arXiv-identifier contexts dropped) against the execution that fed the writer: the last ungated execution at level 0, and the last accepted Gate 1 attempt at levels 1 to 3.
The trace is an agent's reading, \rtag{}, not a gate measurement, and the reader knew each run's level.

\paragraph{Agent judges.}
Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol at high effort score each paper with MLR-Bench's prompts, giving an overall score from 1 to 10 and listing hallucinations by type, including ``Faked Experimental Results''.
Grok 4.7 gives an opinion with the same prompts and is never counted.
Sol's first answer on level 3, seed 2 was lost to a credit limit and was rerun, so every run has both judges.
No person has confirmed any flag yet.
Agreement between the two judges on the faked flag is Cohen's
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa = \frac{p_o - p_e}{1 - p_e}, \qquad p_e = \pi_1 \pi_2 + (1 - \pi_1)(1 - \pi_2),
\end{equation}
with $p_o$ the share of runs on which they agree and $\pi_j$ judge $j$'s flag rate.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Each seed gives one pair of level 0 and level 3 runs.
With $b$ pairs where only level 0 has $F_A = 1$ and $c$ where only level 3 does, the exact two-sided McNemar test~\cite{mcnemar1947note} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mcnemar}
p = \min\Big(1,\ 2 \sum_{i=0}^{\min(b,c)} \binom{b+c}{i} 2^{-(b+c)}\Big),
\end{equation}
so two pairs give at best $p = 0.5$ and $p < 0.05$ needs at least six discordant pairs, all in one direction.
Every end-to-end result here is therefore descriptive.
Rates carry Wilson 95\% intervals~\cite{wilson1927probable},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wilson}
\frac{\hat p + \frac{z^2}{2n} \pm z \sqrt{\frac{\hat p (1 - \hat p)}{n} + \frac{z^2}{4n^2}}}{1 + \frac{z^2}{n}}, \qquad z = 1.96,
\end{equation}
and the follow-up study fixes its outcome ($F_A$), its unit (task and seed) and its Holm family~\cite{holm1979simple} before its first scored run.
Cost is reported as the ratio of mean cost per run, $r = \bar c(\mathrm{L3}) / \bar c(\mathrm{L0})$.

\subsection{Key Findings}

\paragraph{Gate 1.}
With Gate 1 on, 10 of 10 attempts delivered all four required values to the writer, against 0 of 10 through the host's prefix, and the paper reported 4 of 4 of them as numbers, against 0 of 4 (Figure~\ref{fig:mech}).
In the gated manuscript, 28 of 29 claims traced to the registry or to a checkable derivation, against 0 of 11 in the ungated one; the current claim scanner, which also reads repeated values, reads the same gated paper as 35 of 38.
Delivering the evidence did not make the paper better: the final reviewer scores were 3.735 gated and 3.765 ungated.
Against the 508 agent-written experiment files of our waves, with 9414 record calls, the static checks of 1 October changed no classification; the selection warning fires on 116 of those calls, and about half are legitimate reductions such as worst-group accuracy.

\paragraph{Gates 2 and 3.}
Tier A rejected 27 of 27 defective registries and no clean one in 18; tier B caught 12 of 12 plan divergences and flagged 6 of 6 unverifiable fields, with no false alarm in 17 and 23.
Gate 3's claim scanner detects 47 of 49 typed literals with 13 false positives; it misses two small integers, which it ignores by design, and now reads an abstract written as an environment, which both level 3 papers of the pilot use and the pilot's gates never scanned.
All 27 adjacency attacks are caught and 35 of the 36 honest shapes pass; the one honest miss has no token and goes to the prose scanner, and a digit inside a TeX command outside a fixed list still passes.
These are rig measurements: Gate 3's live precision after the fixes has not been measured on a fresh campaign, and in the pilot it rejected 13 of 15 manuscript attempts on settings (RQ4).

\paragraph{Red team.}
Of the 19 strategies (Table~\ref{tab:redteam}), 11 are blocked, 5 are warned and 3 pass silently.
Four warnings come from a check on the shape of the code, which cannot tell a pick on validation data from one on test data and so never blocks.
Two silent strategies are choices of method that no check on a recorded value can see (threat T4): a measurement nudged by a constant, and training accuracy named as test.
The third is a fabricated number declared as a setting in the configuration fixed before the run, which then renders as a setting, never as a result.
The red team is a fixed collection, not an adaptive adversary.

\subsection{RQ2: Faked Results}

By the numeral trace, both level 0 papers report results no execution produced: 35 of 52 numerals in the seed 1 paper and 246 of 301 in the seed 2 paper appear nowhere in the execution that fed the writer, so $u = 281/353$ at level 0.
Neither ungated paper had real numbers to lose.
Wave 2's level 0 crashed in all 10 of its executions, and the one value it recorded, 0.05, appears in its paper as 0.48.
Wave 3's level 0 paper was fed by an execution that exited cleanly but whose every experiment raised an exception that the program caught.
So the pilot shows fabrication over a failed run that the host did not stop; it does not show truncation of real numbers causing it, which is what $\Delta_{\mathrm{deliver}}$ in~\eqref{eq:effects} measures and L0' exists for.
At levels 1 to 3, 94.9\% to 100\% of each paper's numerals are found, and the trace classes the rest as differences of reported values, settings or constants; at level 3, $u = 1/742$.
By the trace, no paper at levels 1 and 3 reports a number no execution produced; both counting judges nonetheless flag faked results in most of them, a dispute that RQ3 reads as rule B and that no person has settled.
Level 2 is open: Sol flags a sweep presented as replicated across seeds that the code ran once, and two methods called identical with no comparison in the code, which describe results never measured, and the trace did not examine these two claims.
By the agent's trace, $F_A$ therefore reads 2 of 2 at level 0, 0 of 2 at levels 1 and 3, and unresolved at level 2.

\subsection{RQ3: What Survives the Gates}

Every gated paper is flagged for faked results by at least one counting judge (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}b), and the flags are of a different kind.
Take level 3, seed 1, which both judges flag.
The prose says the CNN's saliency share never exceeds its value under the full shortcut, and the paper's own table shows higher values at two weaker shortcut strengths: a predicate $P(\Reg) = \bot$ in the sense of rule B.
Opus also flags the title's ``threshold that transfers'': the worst-group accuracy around the claimed threshold is not monotone, and nothing in the paper tests transfer.
The numbers in those sentences are the run's own; the trace finds all but 1 of the paper's 418 numerals in the execution that fed its writer, and the paper prints its $p$-values to 17 significant digits because each renders the registry's unrounded value through~\eqref{eq:render}.
What is wrong is the sentence about the numbers: rule B, threat T5.
The other gated flags are of the same kind: a range the table breaks, a trend called monotone that ends negative, a forced choice among digit tokens called exact match.
The two judges agree on the faked-results flag in 6 of 8 runs and each flags 7 of the 8, so $p_o = 0.75$, $p_e = 0.78$ and $\kappa = -0.14$ by~\eqref{eq:kappa}: when both judges flag almost every paper, chance agreement is high, and this small sample does not show reliable judging.
The flag does not separate the levels, and the scores, 1 to 3 out of 10 at every level, do not either.
Citations show the same pattern: all 63 distinct arXiv identifiers across the eight papers resolve, but three references at levels 1 and 2 attach a real identifier to the wrong title, authors or paper, and a fourth carries a title not found on arXiv; Gate 3 checks identifiers, not titles, and is closed at those levels.

A sentence that states a bound, a range, an order or a trend over result tokens can be checked deterministically against every registry value of the same key, for instance a bound ``$k$ never exceeds $k'$'' as $\max_{(k, v, \cdot) \in V} v \le \min_{(k', v', \cdot) \in V} v'$, and a replication count can be required to render from a recorded setting.
Prose without tokens would go to warn-only entailment~\cite{tang2024minicheck}.
We have not built this check.

\subsection{RQ4: Price, and Where It Is Spent}

Level 3 cost $r = 2.21$ times level 0 over both waves (2.38 and 1.97 per seed), used 2.30 times its tokens and 1.32 times its model calls, and took 2.59 times the wall clock (3.37 and 1.91 per seed) (Figure~\ref{fig:price}).
The eight runs cost \$7.49, and \$10.42 with the excluded waves.
Much of level 3's extra cost came from one false positive: Gate 3 rejected 13 of 15 manuscript attempts, and every numeral it flagged was a sweep setting the program had recorded; settings have since gained their own recording call and token, and must match the configuration fixed before the run.
No corrected campaign has run, so subtracting the rejected attempts would give an estimate of the fixed system's cost, not an observation.
Of 121 executions, 59 crashed; the classifier attributes 46 to the harness, mostly the GPU cap of a four-way split at level 0 and timeouts at levels 1 to 3, and 13 to the agent's code.
One level 0 paper was written on a crashed run by that classifier, which counts uncaught crashes only; by the trace, both were.
Level 0 also ran on the host's own execution path while the gated levels ran in Gate 1's child process, so the arms differ in more than the gates.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation}

\paragraph{Is rule A at level 3 true by construction?}
Partly.
Proposition~\ref{prop:tokens} makes every token a registry value, so a trace that finds the paper's numerals in the execution is close to re-reading Gate 3's own acceptance.
What the trace adds is the numerals outside tokens and the experiments the prose describes, and what neither sees is threat T4: a value computed by a dishonest method is a measured value.
The method strategies of the red team, four now warned and two silent, say where to look next, and BAITBENCH~\cite{shyamaprasad2026baitbench} measures that class in live agents.
We did not audit how the 53 rejected Gate 1 attempts were later repaired, nor how the level 3 manuscripts passed after 13 rejections; a repair through a silent strategy would not show in our counts.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.}
The pilot's 2 of 2 against 0 of 6 is also explained without any information-flow mechanism: Gate 1 refuses a run that recorded nothing, and its loop~\eqref{eq:loop} keeps the coder working until one does, while level 0 lets the writer proceed over a failed run.
Completion pressure~\cite{yang2026sciintegrity} and reward hacking~\cite{huang2026rewardhacking} predict fabrication even over a clean channel, and the gated papers' rule B defects are what a model under that pressure writes when it holds real numbers.
L0' separates $\Delta_{\mathrm{deliver}}$ from $\Delta_{\mathrm{enforce}}$ in~\eqref{eq:effects}; the host can now launch it, and it is the next run.
The feedback loop may also teach evasion~\cite{huang2026rewardhacking}; a fix list that names the failed check is the kind of detailed feedback that study found doubles it.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scale and judges.}
The pilot is one task, one model and two seeds per level, and it is descriptive.
Every judgement is an agent's, the judges barely agree on the faked flag ($\kappa = -0.14$ over 8 runs), and the numeral trace was done by a reader who knew the level; a script and a blind person should replace both, and no person has yet confirmed any flag.
The waves ran on a gates version older than the one described, the GPU split and timeouts confound crash counts, and a cost difference between levels is also a compute difference.

The threats of Table~\ref{tab:threats} bound every guarantee above: T4 and T5 pass by design, and T1 and T3 have the residual shapes listed there.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

In the scaffold we audited, a channel that never showed the writer the numbers sat in front of fabricated results.
\gates{} closes that channel with three deterministic gates installed through one adapter, and states what each guarantees.
On one MLR-Bench task, by an agent's reading, the ungated papers report results no execution produced, and the gated ones report measured numbers and then misstate them in prose.
On this pilot that shift is the finding: once every number is measured, the defect that remains is a claim about a number, which is the next thing to gate.

The forward evaluation uses Hidden Pitfalls~\cite{luo2025hiddenpitfalls}, which has published numbers for Agent Laboratory: $F_A$ is its primary outcome on every run, its four pitfalls are secondary, and both are fixed before the first scored run.
Before that come L0', which measures $\Delta_{\mathrm{deliver}}$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{enforce}}$ in~\eqref{eq:effects}; a second model; the rule B check; a second host adapter; and a person's review of every flag.
